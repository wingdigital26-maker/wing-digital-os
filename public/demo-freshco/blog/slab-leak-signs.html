How to know if you have a slab leak: 7 warning signs

By the FreshCo Plumbing team · Updated July 2026 · 6 min read

Quick answer: The most reliable signs of a slab leak are a sudden jump in your water bill, warm spots on the floor, the sound of running water when every fixture is off, and a water meter that keeps spinning with the house shut down. If you notice two or more of these, get a professional leak detection scheduled this week.

Slab leaks are one of the most common major plumbing problems in Dallas-Fort Worth, and it is not bad luck. Most North Texas homes sit on concrete slab foundations poured over expansive clay soil. That clay swells when it rains and shrinks hard in our summer droughts, and the slab shifts with it. The water lines running under or through that slab get flexed, rubbed, and stressed year after year until one develops a pinhole leak. Older homes with copper lines in Dallas, Plano, Garland, and Fort Worth are especially prone.

The tricky part is that the leak is invisible. Water escapes under the concrete where you cannot see it, sometimes for months. Here are the seven signs we tell every DFW homeowner to watch for.

1. Your water bill jumped and nothing else changed

This is the number one early warning. A pinhole slab leak can lose thousands of gallons a month. If your bill climbed 30 percent or more and you did not fill a pool, host relatives, or start watering the lawn more, take it seriously. Pull your last six bills and compare usage in gallons, not dollars, since rates change too.

2. Warm spots on the floor

About half the slab leaks we repair are on the hot water line. Hot water leaking under the slab heats the concrete above it, so you get a patch of tile or wood floor that feels warm barefoot for no reason. Pets love to nap on these spots, which is a genuinely useful tell. A warm spot plus a higher gas or electric bill (your water heater running constantly) points strongly to a hot side slab leak.

3. The sound of running water when everything is off

Stand in a quiet hallway at night with every fixture and appliance off. A faint hiss or trickle sound coming from the floor or a wall is water escaping somewhere under pressure. This one deserves a same-week phone call.

4. The water meter test fails

You can check this yourself in ten minutes:

  1. Turn off every fixture and water-using appliance, inside and out.
  2. Find your water meter at the street and look at the small leak indicator dial or the digital flow indicator.
  3. If it is moving with the whole house off, water is leaving your system somewhere.
  4. Now shut the valves at your toilets and water heater and check again. Still moving? The leak is likely in a line you cannot see, and under the slab is the prime suspect.

5. Cracks in floors, walls, or tile

Water under a slab erodes and softens the soil supporting it. Over time the slab settles unevenly, and you see it as new cracks in tile, drywall cracks above door frames, or doors that suddenly stick. DFW foundations move seasonally anyway, but new cracking combined with any other sign on this list is a red flag.

6. Damp carpet, buckled wood, or musty smells

When enough water accumulates, it finds its way up through the slab. Unexplained damp patches in carpet, cupping or buckling in wood floors, or a persistent musty smell in one room all suggest moisture rising from below. Mold follows moisture, so do not wait on this one.

7. Low water pressure at every fixture

A significant leak steals pressure from the whole system. If pressure dropped everywhere at once, and your neighbors are fine, water is escaping before it reaches your fixtures.

What happens after a slab leak is confirmed

Modern slab leak repair is far less dramatic than most homeowners fear. Depending on the location and condition of the line, options include a spot repair through a small access opening, rerouting that single line overhead through walls and attic, or, for homes with aging pipe throughout, a whole-home repipe so you never do this twice. A good plumber will explain all applicable options with fixed prices before any demolition happens. That is exactly how our slab leak repair service works.

Frequently asked questions

What does a slab leak sound like?

A faint hissing or running water sound when every fixture is off, usually most noticeable at night near bathrooms or the water heater closet.

Is a slab leak an emergency?

Not same-hour, but it is urgent. Water under the slab erodes supporting soil and can damage the foundation. Schedule detection within days, not weeks.

Does homeowners insurance cover slab leaks in Texas?

It varies. Many Texas policies cover resulting water damage and access costs but not the pipe repair itself. Check your policy and ask your agent specifically about slab and foundation coverage.

How much does slab leak detection cost?

Commonly a few hundred dollars in DFW. Reputable companies, FreshCo included, credit the detection fee toward the repair if you proceed.
