What a water heater replacement costs in DFW (2026 guide)

By the FreshCo Plumbing team · Updated July 2026 · 7 min read · Example figures for demo purposes

Quick answer: In Dallas-Fort Worth, replacing a standard 40 or 50 gallon tank water heater typically costs $1,500 to $3,000 installed. A tankless conversion typically runs $4,000 to $7,500. Where you land in those ranges depends on unit quality, where the heater lives in your house, and how much code-required work comes with it. (Figures are illustrative examples for this demo site.)

Nobody budgets for a water heater. It fails on a Tuesday, you get three wildly different quotes, and you have no idea which one is fair. This guide breaks down what actually drives the price in North Texas so you can read any quote, ours included, with confidence.

These are installed prices: unit, labor, standard code items, haul-away, and permit. If a quote looks dramatically cheaper, something on that list is probably missing.

The five things that move the price

1. Where the unit lives

A garage swap is the easy case. Attic installs, common across Plano, Frisco, and McKinney, add labor, safety pan and drain requirements, and sometimes two technicians to move the unit safely. Expect attic locations to add several hundred dollars.

2. Code upgrades

If your old unit was installed a decade ago, code has moved on. Expansion tanks, sediment traps, drain pans, proper venting clearances, and seismic strapping where required all get brought current during replacement. This is not upselling; the city inspector checks for them.

3. Unit quality and warranty

A 6-year builder-grade tank and a 12-year premium tank can differ by $500 or more in equipment cost. With North Texas hard water working against every tank, we usually think the longer warranty earns its keep.

4. Gas and venting changes

This is the big variable on tankless conversions. Tankless units need more gas volume and different venting than the tank they replace, and running a larger gas line or new stainless vent is real work. It is also why tankless quotes vary the most between homes.

5. Permits and inspection

Most DFW cities require a permit for water heater replacement. It is a modest line item, and a company that skips it is telling you something about how they handle the parts you cannot see.

Rule of thumb: if your tank is over 10 years old or the tank itself is leaking, replace it. If it is younger and the problem is a thermostat, element, or valve, repair it. More detail on that decision on our water heater service page.

Tank vs tankless in North Texas

Tankless wins on lifespan (20+ years vs 8-12), operating cost, and never running out of hot water. Tanks win on upfront price and simplicity. Our honest guidance: households of four or more that regularly run out of hot water tend to be thrilled with tankless. A two-person household replacing a garage unit is usually better served by a quality 50 gallon tank and putting the difference elsewhere.

One DFW-specific note: our hard water means either choice benefits from maintenance. Annual flushing for tanks, and descaling for tankless. Skipping it is the single biggest reason water heaters here die young.

Questions to ask before you approve any quote

  • Is the price flat-rate and final, or an estimate?
  • Does it include permit, haul-away, expansion tank, and pan?
  • What is the warranty on the unit, and separately, on the labor?
  • Who pulls the permit and meets the inspector?
  • Is the installer a licensed plumber or a subcontractor?

Frequently asked questions

How much does a water heater replacement cost in DFW?

Typically $1,500 to $3,000 installed for a standard tank, and $4,000 to $7,500 for a tankless conversion (illustrative demo figures).

Why do water heater quotes vary so much?

Unit quality and warranty, location of the unit, code upgrade items, permit handling, and gas or venting modifications. Compare quotes line by line, not bottom line by bottom line.

Is a tankless water heater worth it in Texas?

For larger households that run out of hot water, usually yes. For small households, a quality tank is often the better value.

How long does a water heater last in North Texas?

8 to 12 years for tanks. Hard water shortens that, and annual flushing extends it.
