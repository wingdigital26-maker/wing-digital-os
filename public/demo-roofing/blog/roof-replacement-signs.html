How to know when your roof needs replacing (8 warning signs)

The short answer, then the details, from roofers who climb DFW roofs every day.

Published July 2026 · 6 min read · Summit Ridge Roofing

The short answer: if your shingles are curling or shedding granules, you can see daylight or water stains in the attic, or the roof is past 15 years old in North Texas conditions, get it inspected now. One or two isolated symptoms usually mean a repair. Several at once usually mean the roof is at the end of its life.

Why DFW roofs age faster than the brochure says

Shingle packaging talks about 25 or 30 year lifespans. That number assumes mild weather. North Texas roofs live through 105 degree summers, rapid heat and cold cycling, straight-line winds, and hail nearly every spring. In practice, most architectural shingle roofs here are done between 15 and 20 years. That context matters when you weigh the signs below.

The 8 warning signs

1. Curling or cupping shingle edges

Walk to the street and look across the roof line in low sun. Shingle edges that lift, curl, or look wavy have lost their oils and flexibility. Curled shingles crack in the next wind event, and there is no repair that reverses it.

2. Granules collecting in gutters and downspouts

A little grit after a new install is normal. Handfuls of black sand in the gutter on a 12 year old roof is the shingle surface washing away. Granules are the sunscreen of your roof. Once they go, the asphalt underneath bakes and cracks quickly.

3. Bald spots or shiny patches

Where granules are already gone, shingles show dark, smooth patches that glint in the sun. Those spots are leaking within a season or two.

4. Missing or wind-lifted shingles after storms

Finding a shingle in the yard once might be a one-off repair. Losing them in every storm means the sealant strips across the whole roof have failed, and each fix is a bandage on a bigger problem.

5. Water stains on ceilings or in the attic

Brown rings on a ceiling, damp insulation, or streaks on the attic side of the decking mean water is already getting through. Catch it early and the fix may be one flashing. Wait a year and you are replacing decking too.

6. Daylight through the roof boards

Go into the attic on a bright day with the lights off. Pinpoints of light through the decking mean gaps that water follows. This is a now problem, not a someday problem.

7. Sagging roof lines

A ridge or plane that dips visibly usually means the decking or the structure under it has absorbed moisture and weakened. This moves the conversation past shingles and needs a professional look promptly.

8. Your neighbors are all re-roofing

DFW subdivisions were shingled street by street, and the same hail hits everyone. When the houses around you built the same year start replacing roofs, yours is living on the same clock. It costs nothing to have that checked.

Repair or replace? A simple test

  • One symptom, roof under 12 years old: almost always a repair.
  • Two or three symptoms, roof 12 to 15 years: get an inspection with photos, and get the honest math on repair cost versus remaining life.
  • Several symptoms, roof past 15 years: replacement is usually the cheaper option per year of service, and you get to choose impact-rated materials that take the next hail season better.

Frequently asked questions

How long does a shingle roof last in Texas?

Typically 15 to 20 years for architectural shingles in North Texas. Sun, heat cycling, and hail shorten the brochure number considerably.

Can I replace just part of my roof?

Small sections can be patched. But partial replacement of an aging roof costs more per square, leaves visible mismatches, and the seams tend to fail early. Past about a third of the roof, full replacement usually wins.

Do I need to replace my roof after hail?

Not always. An inspection with photos shows whether the hail only marked the surface or actually broke the shingle mat. Broken mat leads to leaks and early failure.

How often should I have my roof inspected?

Once a year, plus after any storm that drops hail bigger than a quarter. Summit Ridge inspections are free and come with photos of what we find.
